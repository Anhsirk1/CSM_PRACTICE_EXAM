2. Question
The product backlog is sorted into

Stories
Epics
Sprints
Releases
 Unattempted
✅ C. Sprints

In the context of Agile methodologies, which ServiceNow CSM leverages, the product backlog is prioritized based on value, risk, dependencies, and other factors to determine what work will be undertaken in future sprints.
While the entire backlog isn’t strictly “sorted into” sprints in a persistent, fixed manner, the planning process involves selecting and organizing items from the prioritized backlog into specific sprints for development and delivery. This is the most direct relationship to how the backlog is organized for action.
The sprint backlog, a subset of the product backlog, is what is committed to for a specific sprint. The product backlog itself informs the content of these sprint backlogs.
❌ A. Stories

Stories are individual units of work or requirements within the product backlog. The backlog is composed of stories (and potentially other item types like epics and tasks), but it is not sorted into them.
Stories themselves have attributes like priority, but the backlog’s sorting mechanism is based on these attributes, not by grouping the entire backlog into categories of “stories.”
❌ B. Epics

Epics are larger bodies of work that are typically broken down into smaller stories. While epics help to organize the backlog conceptually, the backlog isn’t fundamentally sorted into epics as the primary sorting mechanism for day-to-day planning and execution.
Epics might span multiple sprints and contain numerous stories, but the backlog’s actionable order is driven by the prioritization of the individual items within it.
❌ D. Releases

Releases are deployments of a set of completed features or functionalities. The product backlog contains the work that will eventually be part of releases, but the backlog itself isn’t sorted into releases as its primary organizational structure.
Release planning happens based on the prioritized items in the backlog and the capacity of development teams over time, potentially spanning multiple sprints.

6. Question
If associated with a case, which of the below can create/cause a blocking task?

Demand
Incident
Problem
Change
SLA
Case Task
 Unattempted
✅ B. Incident

When a case requires technical investigation or resolution that falls under the scope of IT, an incident record can be created and linked to the case. This related incident can often block the resolution of the parent case until the underlying technical issue is resolved. The case agent might need to wait for the incident to be closed before they can provide a final resolution to the customer.
✅ C. Problem

If a case is identified as part of a larger, recurring issue, a problem record might be created to investigate the root cause. This related problem can block the case if the resolution for the individual case depends on the findings and fixes implemented through the problem management process. Case agents might be instructed to hold off on resolving the case until the underlying problem is addressed to prevent recurrence.
✅ D. Change

In situations where resolving a case requires a modification to the IT infrastructure or services, a change request might be initiated and linked to the case. This related change can block the case if the case resolution is dependent on the successful implementation of the change. The case cannot be fully resolved until the necessary changes are made.
✅ F. Case Task

A case task is a sub-task directly related to the case and is used to break down the work required to resolve the case. A case task can be marked as blocking, meaning the parent case cannot be closed until all blocking case tasks are completed. This ensures all necessary steps within the case resolution process are finished.
❌ A. Demand

A demand in ServiceNow typically relates to a request for a new product, service, or enhancement. While a case might lead to the creation of a demand, the demand itself, once created and potentially linked, does not inherently function as a blocking task for the original case in the same way that incidents, problems, or changes do. The progress of the demand is usually tracked separately from the case resolution.
❌ E. SLA

An SLA (Service Level Agreement) defines the expected timelines and quality of service. While an SLA can define deadlines for case resolution and might be impacted by delays caused by related records, the SLA itself does not create or cause a blocking task. It’s a metric against which the case resolution process is measured, not a task that can block it.

 Unattempted
External users, those who don‘t have internal access to the company network, must maintain a minimum role of snc_external to access a ServiceNow instance.

Here‘s why the other options wouldn‘t be suitable:

Customer: While “Customer“ might seem like a logical choice, it‘s not necessarily a universal role name in ServiceNow. The specific role name can vary depending on the instance configuration.
Partner: Partner roles might exist for external companies that collaborate with the organization, but wouldn‘t be the minimum requirement for all external users.
Snc_internal: This role is clearly intended for internal users within the company and wouldn‘t grant access to external users.
